The ground
tilts up

Six weeks of flat coastal walking ended somewhere outside Palamaner without announcing itself. I noticed it in the calves before I noticed it on the horizon.

Log
Distance
23.4 km
Ascent
610 m
Walking
7 h 20
Max temp
36 °C
Slept
Bus shelter

The road out of Chittoor runs straight for eleven kilometres and then begins to bend, and each bend takes a little more than the last one gave back. There is no pass, no signpost, nothing to mark it. The Deccan does not begin anywhere in particular. It simply stops being the coast.

A man on a motorcycle stopped to ask where I was walking to. When I said Kashmir he laughed, then looked at my feet, then stopped laughing. He rode ahead two kilometres and was waiting with a bottle of water when I got there. This has happened eleven times in forty-one days and I have stopped being surprised by it, which feels like a small loss.

The ghat road above Palamaner, 06:40. Full bleed, 12:5. Plate 01

The body has settled into the work. Blisters resolved around day nineteen into something harder. The left knee complains on descents and is silent going up, which is the wrong way round for what is coming.

Forty-one days in, the walk stopped being a plan and became a job.

Nothing about the schedule has changed. Twenty kilometres a day, four in the morning until eleven, then whatever shade can be found until four. What changed is that I no longer negotiate with it. The negotiating was the tiring part.

Tomorrow the road climbs another 400 metres toward Bengaluru, and after that the plateau holds level for nine hundred kilometres — the longest featureless stretch of the whole route. I have been told to dread it. I am, instead, looking forward to being bored.

The man with the water bottle. Plate 02, 4:5.
